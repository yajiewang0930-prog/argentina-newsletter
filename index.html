  • September Inflation Seen at 1.9%; Full-Year Held at 30%

    high

    The BCRA's September Market Expectations Survey (REM), conducted September 28–30, lifted the September inflation estimate to 1.9% month-over-month — one tenth above the prior poll and up from 1.7% in August — with core at 1.8%. Analysts see October at 1.7%, November 1.6% and December 1.8%. The full-year 2026 consensus stays anchored near 30% (the Top-10 forecasts 30.3%), only slightly below 2025's 31.5% and far under August's 33.5% year-over-year print. The official September CPI, due October 13, will test whether the mild reacceleration is temporary. Twelve-month-ahead expectations sit at 21.1%, matching the figure in the 2027 budget bill sent to Congress.

Joblessness captured 19.8% of mentions, up from 17.6% in July, while concern over price and tariff increases fell to 18.9% from 22.4%. It is a notable political moment for the Milei administration, which built its support on taming inflation — a success now increasingly taken for granted as voters turn to jobs and wages. Sustaining disinflation while reigniting hiring has become the central policy challenge heading into the final quarter.

    high

    The government ended the first half with a primary surplus of 0.6% of GDP, narrowly below the 0.7% interim target agreed with the IMF — $6.29 trillion against a $6.86 trillion goal, a gap of roughly 0.1% of GDP. Presidential spokesperson Adrián Ravier said Argentina "over-delivered" many targets and did not rule out requesting a waiver for the small deviation, an option under active discussion as the third review concludes. A clean review would unlock a US$900 million disbursement and keep the 2027 financing path on schedule.

🏦 Financial & Credit

The BCRA launched "Fase 2" of its stabilization program — selling dollars to drain peso liquidity — as household delinquency set another record. Rates held elevated, and the central bank's reserve accumulation remained the program's strongest pillar.

  • BCRA Launches "Fase 2": Dollar Sales to Drain $2.4tn Pesos

    high

    The BCRA under Santiago Bausili unveiled the "Fase 2" of its stabilization program, aiming to withdraw up to $2.4 trillion pesos (nearly US$1.9–2 billion) by selling dollars in the MEP and CCL markets to engineer a scarcity of pesos. The move pairs with a tax-treatment change on LEFIs that lifts the net policy rate perceived by banks from 3.00% to 3.34% monthly, and caps the broad monetary base at $47.7 trillion, about 9.1% of GDP. Officials want to keep short-term peso rates firm without a formal reference-rate hike. Markets read the package as a tightening signal, though bank deposit and lending rates had yet to fully react by early October.

  • Household Delinquency Sets Record at 12.9%

    high

    Bank credit irregularity among households reached 12.9% in July, the highest since 2004 and more than double the 5.6% of a year earlier, according to the Fundación Pensar "Pensar Argentina" report. Personal loans show the worst deterioration at 16.7%, followed by credit cards at 12.8% and UVA-adjusted loans at 20.3%; mortgages remain healthy at 1.6%. August brought a first dip in more than two years — back to 12.8% — though levels remain near record highs. The BCRA attributes the climb to the rapid expansion of credit after years of low financial intermediation and notes provisions cover 87.6% of irregular loans, keeping the system solvent even as borrowers struggle.

The BCRA's injection of $545 billion pesos through recent dollar purchases added liquidity, but banks have been slow to pass lower funding costs to borrowers.

    high

    The BCRA has purchased more than US$14.2 billion in the foreign-exchange market so far in 2026, surpassing the US$10 billion annual target by over US$4.2 billion — the program's clearest over-performance. 💳 Fintech

The digital-credit crackdown escalated as Buenos Aires Province moved against six platforms, while the BCRA confirmed Mercado Pago will become a bank before year-end. Fintech default far outpaces the regulated system, deepening the fight over who supervises digital lenders.

  • BCRA Confirms Mercado Pago Will Operate as a Bank by Year-End

    high

    The BCRA confirmed Mercado Pago will operate as a bank before the end of the year, with board member Juan Curutchet pointing to November for approval of the license — following the authorizations already granted to Cocos and Revolut. The conversion from CVU to CBU means users' balances shift from money-market fund shares to insured bank deposits, with the full backing of the deposit-guarantee system. With more than 25 million users, Mercado Pago would gain access to payroll accounts — a monthly wage mass estimated at $20 trillion — and could scale retail credit, mortgages and SME banking. The move has already generated tension with traditional banks.

  • Buenos Aires Province Probes Six Fintechs; Fines Up to $1,883M

    high

    Governor Axel Kicillof and production minister Augusto Costa opened an investigation into Mercado Pago, Naranja X, Ualá, Personal Pay, Brubank and Cencosud after 2,240 consumer-protection complaints in the first half of 2026. The province demands the firms explain how they set interest rates and the true composition of their Costo Financiero Total, why similar borrowers receive different rates under algorithmic pricing, and whether a visible "repentance" button exists. Officials flagged cases where charges and penalties push effective annual rates above 1,000%. If infractions are confirmed, fines could reach $1,883 million, plus orders to reverse the practices. The province regulates under consumer-protection law, while macroprudential authority remains with the BCRA.

  • IMF Third Review Concludes; $900M Disbursement Pending

    high

    The IMF's technical mission, led by Joyce Wong, concluded the third review of the Extended Fund Facility after working in Buenos Aires September 21–29. No staff-level agreement was announced; talks will continue "in the coming weeks," and any disbursement still requires Executive Board approval. At stake is roughly US$900 million (some sources cite US$869 million) that would rebuild reserves after a recent US$795–800 million repayment. Spokesperson Julie Kozack praised "notable progress" on stability while urging broader-based growth. Economy minister Luis Caputo heads to Bangkok October 12–18 for the IMF–World Bank annual meetings, where he will meet managing director Kristalina Georgieva to try to close the review.

In the first half of 2026 the oil-and-petrochemical complex exported US$8.12 billion, up 43.7% year-over-year, narrowing the gap to soy's US$9.08 billion (18.3% of total exports) to just US$966 million.
